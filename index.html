Care at the end of the road.

We are a rural health provider. We hold the contracts, hire the care managers out of the counties they serve, and run the technology that lets one nurse carry a panel of 700 instead of 200.

Talk to us What we do

What we actually do.

Three capabilities, run as one business. Rural care management fails on any one of them alone, which is why this is a company rather than a staffing agency with software attached.

Contracts and capture

We win and hold the contracts. Where a vehicle is reserved for a certified small business, our delivery partner bids it instead.

Care delivery

Promotoras and nurse care managers, hired out of the counties they cover, billing chronic care management at $66.13 per patient per month and community health integration at $86.17.

Technology

myabuela calls on an ordinary telephone, in the patient’s own language, every morning. No app, no smartphone, no broadband.

Markets we serve.

We build one market at a time, and we start where the need is measurable and the money is already appropriated. Two of these are live or early. The other three are where we go next. They show how a market gets picked. They are not the business.

New Mexico

Live since 2026

$211.5m awarded across four initiatives, six Regional Hubs holding the budget and no staff behind it. We work the frontier and tribal counties in the north and west, out of a principal office in San Miguel County. Nearly a quarter of households here answer the phone in Spanish, which is why the product was built the way it was.

Mississippi

Target

One of the three most rural states in the country, and the one where the hospital arithmetic is worst. Roughly half of its rural hospitals are at risk of closing. The Delta counties carry chronic disease at the top of the national range, and the state did not expand Medicaid, so an enrolled panel here has a larger uninsured share than anywhere else we look.

Target
